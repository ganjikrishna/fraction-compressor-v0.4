# Review: Fraction Compressor v0.4

## 1. Summary

Fraction Compressor v0.4 is a tool that converts an existing fine-tuned model into a tiny, swappable patch file against its base model. Instead of storing the full weight delta (nearly model-sized), it factorizes the delta into two low-rank matrices whose structure is kept as 1-bit signs, priced end-to-end at roughly 0.31 bits per weight on the Qwen2.5-0.5B pair. The patch is applied at runtime via two small matrix multiplies (Y = (XU)Vᵀ) without ever materializing the full delta, enabling live hot-swapping of behaviors in ~0.1 seconds. The project demonstrates this on four base/Instruct pairs (0.5B–1.5B parameters), using an LLM-driven recipe search (Muse Spark) to allocate per-layer rank budgets under a hard byte constraint. The central claim: useful fine-tune behavior survives aggressive compression, but a hard limit exists for skills gated by the first output token.

## 2. Core Idea

A fine-tuned model = base model + delta (weight difference). The delta is factorized per layer as Δ ≈ U Vᵀ where U, V are low-rank. The *structure* of U and V (their signs) is stored as 1-bit values; scales and factors are stored at higher precision. The total storage (signs + factors + scales) comes to ~0.28–0.31 bits/weight depending on layer size and rank. At inference, the patch is applied as two skinny multiplies against the activations, so the base weights never change and patches swap by loading a different ~20–40 MB file. Compression is a one-time offline pass (subtract models, factorize, quantize signs); the runtime is training-free and zero-overhead beyond the two multiplies.

The novelty the authors claim is not the 1-bit factored format itself (prior art exists) but: (a) an in-loop recipe search where an LLM proposes per-layer rank allocations under a byte budget and later judges results against exact result files; (b) the forced-multiply runtime that never reconstructs the full matrix; (c) a first-token gate diagnosis with a force-start control on one JSON-extraction skill; (d) a verification harness with independent recomputation, cross-model judging, and a quantum-random audit sample.

## 3. Main Results

All numbers below are taken verbatim from the README's benchmark section. "Gap closed" = share of (base → full-patch score gap) recovered by the compressed patch. Scores are log-probability margins (higher = better). Fresh questions = 96 held-out items generated by Muse Spark, split 24 original / 72 new.

| Model Pair | Headline Recipe (bytes) | Human Uniform Recipe (bytes) | Gap Closed (Headline) | Gap Closed (Human) | Notes |
|------------|-------------------------|------------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|-------|
| **Qwen2.5-0.5B** | mlp_behemoth_lite (19,358,176) | rank-32 uniform (19,434,496) | **23.2%** (96 fresh) | 2.1% | Headline wins both halves: 9.5% vs 1.6% (orig 24), 30.4% vs 2.3% (new 72). |
| **SmolLM2-360M** | o-down-rebalanced (19,683,328) | rank-32 uniform (19,705,856) | **>100%** (compressed > full patch) | >100% | Raw margin: +0.293 score points for headline. % not a true recovery share. |
| **Llama 3.2 1B** | no_embed_mlp_spread_balanced (24,515,584) | rank-32 uniform (25,143,296) | **15.9%** | **−7.6%** (worse than base) | Headline wins both halves: 20.0% vs −1.7%, 14.8% vs −9.1%. First-token accuracy: 2.1% (headline) vs 21.9% (full). |
| **Qwen2.5-1.5B** | Down Dominator (38,623,232) | rank-32 uniform (39,046,144) | **>100%** (130.7% vs 127.9%) | >100% | Raw margin: +0.13 pts. Headline loses first 24, wins new 72. First-token accuracy: 26.0% vs 32.3% (human) vs 33.3% (full). |

**BitDelta baseline (Qwen 0.5B, ~62 MB floor):** Plain 1-bit delta (sign × per-matrix mean |Δ|, no training) closes **43.6%** vs factored uniform rank-108 at matched price (61.3 MB) closing **38.5%**. Below ~62 MB the factored format wins by *reach* (plain format cannot go smaller); at ~62 MB the plain initialization wins on quality.

**First-token gate limit (NuExtract-1.5-tiny on Qwen 0.5B):** Full fine-tune 9/10, base 0/10, rank-32 patch 1/10, rank-64 2/10, rank-128 2/10. Force-start control: forced base 7/10 clean, forced patch 8/10 clean + 1 partial (matches fine-tune). Recipe concentrating budget in embedding + attention output: 0/10. Conclusion: the skill is mostly in the base; the fine-tune mainly installs the "start" decision; the compressed patch rarely flips that one token.

## 4. Strengths

- **Honest, reproducible evaluation.** Every headline number is script-produced, cross-checked by independent models (Gemini arithmetic, Muse Spark judging, Muse Glimmer wording rules), and a quantum-random 41/41 audit passed. The README explicitly flags where percentages mislead (SmolLM2, Qwen 1.5B) and gives raw margins instead.
- **Clear articulation of a real limit.** The first-token gate diagnosis is unusually thorough: it isolates the failure mode (start-vs-stop decision), runs a force-start control, tests rank scaling (32→128), tests recipe reallocation, and cites two 2026 papers (Steering Externalities, ACE-Compression) finding the same gate structure. This is not a vague "it doesn't work on some tasks"—it's a characterized boundary.
- **Practical runtime.** The forced-multiply factored apply (two small GEMMs, no full-matrix rebuild) enables genuine hot-swapping in ~100 ms. This is a systems win, not just a compression ratio.
- **Recipe search with byte fairness.** The "Alien Sweep" explicitly penalizes byte-unfair winners and reports byte-fair variants. The Qwen 0.5B headline recipe *beats* the human uniform recipe *at a slightly lower byte count* (19.36 MB vs 19.43 MB).
- **Clean-room implementation.** No third-party code; all methods reimplemented from papers. Prior art is cited precisely with arXiv IDs.

## 5. Weaknesses and Open Questions

- **Gap closure is modest in absolute terms.** 23.2% on the best pair (Qwen 0.5B) means >75% of the fine-tune's quality gain is lost. On Llama 1B it's 15.9%; on the 1.5B scale test the AI recipe's edge shrinks to 0.13 raw score points and doesn't hold on the original 24 questions. The method is clearly "patch for cheap switching," not "patch as fine-tune replacement"—but the README's "breaks the 1-bit bound" framing can overstate the practical significance.
- **First-token gate is a structural blind spot.** The NuExtract test shows that *any* patch at this compression level (up to rank 128, ~72 MB) fails to flip the start token reliably. The force-start control confirms the knowledge is in the base; the patch just doesn't move the gate. This is a format-level limitation for any skill where the first token is a hard commit (refusal, format adherence, tool calls). The authors acknowledge it but offer no mitigation within the format.
- **Recipe search is opaque and compute-heavy.** Muse Spark proposes recipes, judges them, and generates the fresh questions. The search space, number of proposals, and compute cost are not disclosed. "LLM proposes per-layer ranks" is a thin description; without knowing the search budget, it's hard to assess whether the gains are from clever allocation or just more search.
- **No comparison to LoRA or adapter distillation at matched bytes.** The README contrasts with LoRA conceptually ("adapter is a new add-on trained for one task") but never benchmarks a LoRA trained *into* the same byte budget. The relevant baseline for "existing fine-tune → small patch" would be: distill the fine-tune into a LoRA of equal size, or train a LoRA from scratch at that budget. Absent that, we don't know if the factored delta is better than just training a small adapter.
- **Scale test is only one step (0.5B → 1.5B).** The advantage narrows sharply; we have no data at 3B, 7B, or 70B. The "mixed" verdict at 1.5B could become "no advantage" at 3B. The BitDelta baseline also only runs on Qwen 0.5B.
- **Verification harness leans on the same AI ecosystem.** Muse Spark judges, Muse Glimmer sets wording rules, Gemini checks arithmetic—all are Google/Meta-affiliated models. The quantum-random audit (41/41) is a nice touch but only covers a sample of arithmetic, not the recipe search or the qualitative gate diagnosis.
- **No ablation of the factored runtime vs. full-matrix apply.** The forced-multiply path is claimed as a contribution, but there's no measurement of its numerical error vs. materializing Δ and adding it. The `quickstart.py` prints "factored error next to the best any factored form could do at the same rank," but that's not the same as error vs. the uncompressed delta.

## 6. Positioning vs. Related Work

| Prior Work | What It Does | How Fraction Compressor v0.4 Differs |
|------------|--------------|--------------------------------------|
| **BitDelta** (NeurIPS 2024) | 1-bit sign + per-matrix scale for whole delta. | BitDelta's floor is 1 bit/weight (~62 MB on Qwen 0.5B). Fraction Compressor goes below that via factored low-rank + 1-bit signs (~0.31 bits/weight, ~19 MB). At BitDelta's floor, BitDelta wins on quality (43.6% vs 38.5%); Fraction Compressor wins on *reach* (exists at sizes BitDelta cannot reach). |
| **Delta-CoMe** (NeurIPS 2024) | Mixed-precision SVD of delta, training-free. | Shares the "where bytes go matters" insight. Fraction Compressor uses a fixed factored form + LLM recipe search for allocation; Delta-CoMe uses SVD spectrum. No direct benchmark comparison. |
| **D-QRELO** (arXiv:2604.16940) | 1-bit sign×scale pass + truncated SVD of residual. | Closest published relative: sign part + low-rank part. Fraction Compressor merges them into one factored quantized form and adds the recipe search + forced-multiply runtime. |
| **DeltaDQ** (arXiv:2410.08666) | Group sparsification + quantization, 16–512×. | Different compression pathway (sparsity + quantization). No direct comparison. |
| **LittleBit / LittleBit-2** (NeurIPS 2025, ICML 2026) | Factored dual-residual low-bit quantization for *whole models*, with training. | LittleBit compresses whole models with training; Fraction Compressor compresses *post-hoc deltas* training-free. LittleBit-2 rotates factors before quantizing; Fraction Compressor does not (signs are raw). |
| **GraftLLM SkillPack / Skill Weaving SkillZip** (arXiv:2505.18502, 2605.22205) | Package a capability as a compressed delta file, trained into that form. | Fraction Compressor *converts an existing fine-tune* after the fact, no retraining. SkillPack/SkillZip require training the skill in compressed form. |
| **APQF** (arXiv:2608.05499) | LLM planner picks per-layer bit-widths for vision models, whole-model, training-aware. | Fraction Compressor's recipe search is narrower: post-hoc delta, per-layer *rank* (not bit-width), training-free, judged by the same LLM. |

The project's claimed original contributions are narrowly scoped: the *specific form* of the recipe search/judge loop, the forced-multiply runtime, the first-token gate diagnosis with force-start control, and the verification harness. It does not claim the 1-bit factored format itself.

## 7. Overall Verdict

Fraction Compressor v0.4 is a carefully engineered, honestly reported system for turning existing fine-tunes into tiny, hot-swappable patches. Its factored 1-bit format reaches compression ratios (50–80×) that plain 1-bit deltas cannot, and the forced-multiply runtime makes patch swapping practically instant. The benchmark is unusually transparent: it flags where its own metrics mislead, characterizes a sharp first-token gate failure mode with a clean control experiment, and provides a two-budget honesty curve against the simplest rival (BitDelta). However, the absolute gap closure remains modest (15–23% on the two pairs where the metric is valid), the AI recipe's advantage vanishes at 1.5B parameters, and the first-token gate is a structural limitation for any commit-on-first-token skill (refusal, structured output, tool use). The recipe search is a black box with undisclosed compute cost, and no baseline compares against training a LoRA/adapter directly into the same byte budget. The work is a solid systems contribution with a well-marked research boundary, not a breakthrough in compression quality.

**Bottom line:** A credible, reproducible tool for ultra-small behavior patches that work for broad instruction-following but fail on first-token-gated skills, with diminishing recipe gains as model scale grows.

## Notes on accuracy

- Its numbers match the project records: Qwen pair 23.2% vs 2.1% gap closed at 19,358,176 vs 19,434,496 bytes, Llama 15.9% vs uniform's negative result, 1.5B margin of 0.134 nats, BitDelta baseline 43.6% vs 38.5% at matched bytes, and the force-start counts (forced base 7/10, forced patch 8/10 plus 1 partial) all check against the README and result files.
- Two small glosses: its verdict says the recipe advantage "vanishes" at 1.5B, where its own results table shows the recipe still won the combined 96 by 0.134 nats (it lost the 24-question subset only); and its "2.1%" Llama first-token figure is the headline recipe's number, where the best factored config reached 3.1%.
- Its weakness list overlaps the project's own published limits (first-token gate, modest absolute gap closure, scale uncertainty) and adds two the project had not foregrounded: no matched-byte LoRA or adapter-distillation baseline, and the recipe search's compute cost is undisclosed. Its ecosystem point also lands: the validators are Meta and Google models, the same families that built and judged the loop.
